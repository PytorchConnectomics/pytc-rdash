# Research question, evidence, and proposed next test

## Question and decision

State the scientific uncertainty, current evidence/baseline, and what a useful answer would let the project decide. Link the project objective and adopted method. Distinguish a discovery-oriented review from a request to choose an executable experiment.

## Source findings

For relevant candidate explanations or methods, record the source, claim, supporting evidence, limitations, and applicability to this project. Separate published claims, reproduced findings, and hypotheses. Preserve conflicting evidence rather than manufacturing agreement.

## Candidate tests

Describe the observation each test could distinguish, its implementation needs, and its expected information or benefit. Estimate costs in applicable units with their basis; unknown bounds or effect sizes stay unknown. Use quantitative ranking only when the quantities are meaningfully comparable.

For a proposed gate, identify its type, validity/calibration evidence, cost justification, failure implications, and continuation rule. A direct bounded experiment can be appropriate when no informative preliminary gate exists. Check relevant project constraints within their stated applicability and reopening conditions.

## Recommendation

Recommend the next useful test, further evidence needed, or a reason to stop/defer. An exploratory review need not force an experiment merely to satisfy a document quota. If a test is ready, produce a preregistration with separate engineering acceptance, scientific decision rules, and operational stop conditions, within the task's existing authority and budget. Research plans and method proposals do not themselves authorize execution or change portfolio priorities.
